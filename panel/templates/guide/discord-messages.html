Owners can change what the bot posts in Discord from Discord in the top menu. No code and no restart: publish, and the bot updates Discord within a minute.

The Discord page lists every page in groups: What new members see, What the bot posts by itself, Posting yourself, Staff jobs and Setup. Each has a line on what it's for and a status: Live, Draft not published, Bot's default (never changed), or Problem if the bot couldn't do it. ← Discord at the top of any page takes you back.

Picking a channel

Pages for something the bot keeps in a channel have a Channel box at the top: Server info & rules, Factions, Match alerts, Weekly top 3, Staff alerts, Feedback and Link requests.

  1. Pick the channel from the list. The first option is what the bot does with none picked, like its own #servers channel.
  2. It saves as soon as you pick it (the Change channel button does the same). It is separate from Publish: publishing the page doesn't change the channel.
  3. Within a minute the bot takes its message down from the old channel and puts it up in the new one.

The bot uses a picked channel as it is: it doesn't rename it or change who can see it. Make sure it can post there, and that a staff channel (staff alerts, feedback, link requests) really is staff only. Start here, Join greeting, Ban messages and Posts pick their channel inside the page, as part of what you publish.

Editing the Start here message

  1. Go to Discord → Start here message. The editor starts with exactly what's in Discord now.
  2. Under Where and how it looks, pick the channel, and change the title and the colour of the stripe down the side.
  3. Under Text, edit the sections. Each has an optional heading. **bold**, *italic* and new lines work like in Discord. Use ↑ ↓ to reorder, ✕ to remove, and + Add section for more.
  4. Watch the Preview as you type. It's roughly how it'll look in Discord.

Buttons

Each button shows as one line: which line of buttons it's on, its colour, its label and what it does. Tap one to edit it; tap its header again to close it. + Add button makes a new one. Up to 5 buttons fit on each of 5 lines.

For the role, pick one of your Discord roles from the list, or choose + Make a new role… and type a name; the bot makes it when you publish. Tick Only for players who've linked to keep a role for linked players.

A button can never hand out a role that has permissions (admin, kick, manage anything). Those are greyed out in the list. The bot's own role also has to sit above any role it hands out: if it doesn't, the page tells you.

Draft, then publish

  • Save draft keeps your changes without touching Discord. Other owners see the draft too. Discard draft goes back to what's live.
  • Publish sends it to the bot. The box at the top then shows when it went up and who published it, or what went wrong (a missing role, the bot's role too low, a channel it can't post in).
  • If something needs fixing, publishing stops and lists what to change, and your work is kept as a draft.
  • Start again from the original puts the editor back to the bot's original Start here message.

Server info & rules

Discord → Server info & rules edits the #servers card: its title and intro, each server's settings (map, player slots, anything players should know), and the in-game rules card under it. The boxes start with what's in Discord now; a box left as it is keeps following the bot's own wording.

Ban messages

Discord → Ban messages sets what a banned player is told and what staff see in their ticket:

  • The DM: on or off, the title and message (tap {server}, {account} or {length} to drop those in), and how to appeal. Pick a channel from the list to add a link to it, like your ticket channel. The reason and when the ban ends are always added underneath.
  • Ban card in tickets: on or off, the channel with your ticket panel, and any other categories tickets land in. Add your panel's address to put a link to the player's page on the card. Pick In a private thread in the ticket, only for one role and choose a role (like Ticket Manager) to keep the card from the player: the bot opens a private 🔒 Ban info thread in the ticket and adds only that role.

Posts

Discord → Posts is for anything else you want the bot to post and keep up to date: announcements, event sign-ups, FAQs, extra rules.

  1. Press + New post.
  2. Pick the channel, then write it like the Start here message: title, colour, sections of text.
  3. Add buttons if you want: give or take a role (e.g. "I'm in" for an event), pick one role from a group, open a web link, or the same Link Reforger account and My progress buttons as Start here (handy for a link-your-account channel).
  4. Press Publish. To change it later, open it from the list, edit, and publish again: the same message is updated, not a new one posted.
  5. Delete post takes it down from Discord.

Server names

Discord → Server names changes what each server is called everywhere the bot names it: the SERVER STATUS channels, the #servers card, match alerts and results, and the match-notification buttons.

{{ shot("discord-names", "Renaming server 3, with the preview on the right.") }}
  1. Type the new name in the server's box. An empty box keeps the default shown under the server.
  2. Check the preview, then press Publish.

Two servers can't share a name, and a name can't contain · (the bot uses it in the channel names). Discord only lets a bot rename a channel about every 5 minutes, so a SERVER STATUS channel can take that long; everything else changes within a minute.

Factions

Discord → Factions changes what the three factions are called in Discord, their colour and their emoji. The name and colour are the faction's role, so members who picked one change with it. The faction picker's buttons and the /rank card pick up the new name too.

{{ shot("discord-factions", "Renaming FIA to Resistance, with the preview on the right.") }}

It only changes Discord. Which side someone plays in game, rank names and the rank card artwork stay as they are. The bot can only rename or recolour a role that sits below its own; the page tells you if one doesn't. The Start here message's faction buttons have their own labels, set on that page.

Match alerts

Discord → Match alerts sets the "match started" post: which servers post one, whether it pings the players who asked for that server with the 🔔 buttons (untick to post quietly), and the title and message. {server} becomes the server's name. When the match started, and when the alert disappears (30 minutes later), are always added.

Weekly top 3

Every Monday at midnight Eastern the weekly leaderboard starts again, and the bot posts last week's top 3 with their kills. Discord → Weekly top 3 turns it on or off and sets the wording above and below the top 3. {week} becomes the date the week started. It goes in the channel picked at the top, or the weekly leaderboard channel if none is picked. Nobody is pinged.

Staff alerts

Discord → Staff alerts sets when a teamkill burst is posted for staff: how many teamkills, within how many seconds, and an optional role to ping (type its name, like Admin; blank pings nobody). Pick the channel at the top first: until one is picked, alerts wait. Recent alerts lists the last ones sent.

AP mine spam has its own part on the same page: Post AP mine alerts on or off, how many explosive kills to the feet one player needs in one game (until the server restarts), and its own role to ping. It goes in the same channel, once per player per game.

Feedback

Members can send feedback from a Give feedback button. It works like a simple ticket:

  1. On Discord → Feedback, pick the staff channel it goes to at the top. It saves as soon as you pick it.
  2. Under Topics they pick from, put one topic per line (like Servers, Admins, Discord, Bug, Suggestion) and they'll choose from a dropdown. Leave it empty and they type their own.
  3. Set what they're told after sending, the DM they get when it's dealt with, and a role to ping if you want. Press Publish.
  4. Make a post with the button: Discord → Posts → + New post, pick your #feedback channel, write a line or two, and add a button that does Give feedback. Publish it.

Pressing the button asks what it's about (your topics, or their own words) and for their feedback. It shows up in the staff channel with their name, and they can send one every 5 minutes. Each one has two buttons:

  • Reply by DM: type a reply and the bot DMs it to them. It's noted on the feedback, or marked not delivered if their DMs are closed.
  • Done: turns it green, notes who dealt with it, and sends them the thank-you DM.

Make the staff channel one only staff can see: anyone who can see it can press the buttons. Untick Take feedback to close the form for a while.

Channels & roles

Discord → Channels & roles is for the things with no page of their own: the live match board, match results, the weekly leaderboard and rank promotions, and the member roles. Channel changes there apply within a minute of publishing; role changes need the bot restarting. Picked on their own page at the top shows which channel each of the other pages is using, with a link to change it.

Join greeting

{{ shot("discord-greeting", "The join greeting.") }}
  1. Go to Discord → Join greeting and tick Greet them.
  2. Choose to post it in a channel, or send it to them privately.
  3. Write the message. Tap {user}, {name}, {server}, {members} or {start} to drop in their mention, their name, the Discord's name, the member count, or a link to the Start here channel.
  4. Press Publish.

The bot only hears about people joining if Server Members Intent is on for it in Discord's developer portal and ENABLE_MEMBERS_INTENT=1 is in its settings. The page warns you if not.

Link requests

Discord → Link requests is the same queue as the staff link-request channel in Discord. Deciding in either place does the same thing, and the other one catches up.

{{ shot("discord-links", "Two members waiting: one to approve, one whose account needs picking. Everyone already linked is underneath.") }}
  1. Approve links them and gives them the member role. Make sure it's really them first.
  2. If no game account matched the name they typed, start typing their in-game name, pick them from the list and press Link.
  3. Unlink, under Linked accounts, takes their rank roles away. Their playtime stays with the game account.

The bot carries out each decision within a minute. Recent decisions shows whether it worked and, if not, why.
